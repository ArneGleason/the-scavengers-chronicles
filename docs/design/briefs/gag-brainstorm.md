# Gag Brainstorm Brief: The Scavenger's Chronicles

*For an outside brainstorming partner (written to be pasted into ChatGPT). 29 September 2026.*

## What I'm asking you for

I'm building **The Scavenger's Chronicles**, a browser game. It's a ridiculously slapstick, crude, interactive comedy about Bill, a man who has spent forty years preparing to write a synth masterpiece and has written none of it.

I need **gags**: lots of small, physical, silly moments that fill the time between plot beats, plus ideas for the short action challenges at the big beats.

**Please start with our history.** Before you brainstorm, search your memory of our past conversations for anything related to:

- Bill, Rob, *Whiskey Runner Rob*, *Mega Vegas Elvis*, *Keith Richards: Psychic Detective*, *Captain Caffeine*, "Bill's Legal Department", the soup, Big Wanda, Gary the Rummager, Aximandra the cat, and *The Scavenger's Chronicles* itself.
- Running jokes, catchphrases, bits, character quirks, and stories that belong to this circle of friends.

List what you find first, briefly. Then use it: the best gags will be the ones only this group would get. Mark each idea as **[history]** if it comes from our past conversations or **[new]** if you invented it.

## The game in one paragraph

It's 2026. Bill, 54, lives in his late mother's 1955 house, now a hoarder shambles. Everything in it is 1950s because he never replaces anything, and he keeps adding old things to the heaps for their future use. He calls himself an **archivist**. The basement is a 1986 synth altar buried in newspapers, cables and "essential" junk. His taste froze at 14, the year he bought a DIN sync cable he has needed ever since. He's certain the right cable, stump or adapter will finally unlock the masterpiece. The player walks Bill through a series of errands around his house and a Toronto-style west-end neighbourhood. The game ends when he escapes in a private jet to Las Vegas, where the masterpiece finally plays: eight bars long.

The world is 3D, seen from a fixed isometric camera, drawn like a ligne claire comic (Tintin-style clean ink lines, flat colour). At normal zoom Bill is about 110 pixels tall on a 1080p screen, so gags must read from his **whole-body silhouette and big actions**, not facial subtlety.

## The comedy rules (please follow these)

1. **The gag clock.** Something funny happens at least every 10–15 seconds. If the player hasn't caused a gag, the world supplies one.
2. **Easy and guided.** One main plot, one objective always on screen. Nothing is a puzzle you can get stuck on. Every challenge can be won by mashing a button enthusiastically.
3. **Failing is funnier than winning.** Losing flings Bill into the bins; it never sends him back to a checkpoint. Retries are instant.
4. **Traversal is a toy.** Getting from A to B should be something the player fools around with.
5. **Slapstick is physical.** Pratfalls, faceplants, butt-flops, getting flung, stars around the head, sound-effect lettering ("THWACK!"), and a freeze-frame on every impact.
6. **Crude, not cruel.** Crude, absurd and warm, fondly exaggerated, never mean-spirited.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Farts, burps, nose-picking, bird poop, garbage, profanity on signs, bodily indignity | Anything sexual, slurs, or jokes about anyone's real health, money, age, loneliness or looks for their own sake |

The jokes land on Bill's **logic and obsessions**: hoarder reasoning, legal grandeur, soup priorities, cable faith. Bill loses his dignity constantly; he never loses the fight with the world for good. He gets small, absurd victories.

## Bill

- **Look:**
  - Long stringy brown hair hanging in curtains, thin on top with a heroic comb-over.
  - Black sunglasses always on, even indoors. His eyes show only when the glasses slide down his nose, which is his biggest reaction.
  - Red plaid flannel shirt, charcoal sweater vest, corduroys, worn-down loafers.
  - A permanent stoop, a slight belly, big hands, big shoes.
  - A tan canvas satchel across his body with cable ends poking out.
- **Movement:** a scuffing shuffle by default; a panicked, elbows-up "hurry" when running.
- **Props and habits:**
  - Soup from an enamel bowl marked BILL ONLY.
  - An old hairspray can for the side curtains (a background gag only, not a mechanic).
  - Stacks of *Mega Vegas Elvis* comics, an ELVIS LIVES ashtray, and a typewriter for his legal correspondence.
- **Voice:** a legal letter written by a 14-year-old. Deadpan, formal, full of grievance, never embarrassed.
  - Canon line: *"I demand $50 million in compensation. Otherwise, I shall be forced to purchase a second typewriter ribbon."*
  - Nothing he owns is junk. Everything is an asset, an exhibit or a future project.
  - He escalates small matters to historic or legal stakes: a cable is a pilgrimage; a stump is a movement.
  - He suspects he's being left out, by Rob and sometimes by the narrator. He talks back to the narrator, rarely.
- **Game lines, for calibration:**
  - "Exhibit A." (picking up the cable)
  - "My satchel is at capacity. I am consulting counsel." (Counsel is the cat.)
  - "Noted. My legal department will be in touch."
  - "Another step toward the masterpiece. Which is going fine."
- **Narrator voice, for calibration** (these lines are from the game):
  - "He remembers buying it in 1986 and needing it since 1987."
  - "The stump is now furniture-adjacent, which is legally different from garbage."
  - "Gary refuses access to the alphabet. Distract the man, then rob the concept of learning."

## The cast

- **Aximandra.** Bill's tabby cat, the only one who "gets him" (per the note on the fridge). Her ears go flat a few seconds before every soup disaster, and nobody heeds the warning: she's a Cassandra. She sits on exactly the item Bill needs, and she rides to the airfield in the satchel.
- **Gary the Rummager.** A hunched rival scavenger in an olive parka who guards the corner-store dumpster "like a landfill dragon with reading software". He shadows Bill, gets distracted by a humming power brick, and fights Bill for an antique Speak & Spell.
- **Big Wanda.** Runs the city landfill. She has aggressive admiration for Bill's salvage taste and wants to capture him; it's a chase.
- **Bylaw officers.** Patrol the laneway and the lot with flashlights. Bill answers scolding with legal threats.
- **The gym guys.** From the boxing gym on the lane. They laugh off Bill's insults.
- **Rob.** Bill's friend from *Whiskey Runner Rob* and the *Mega Vegas Elvis* comics. Rob keeps promising Bill a bigger part. Running canon: "Bill begins to suspect Rob may have oversold his role." A possible cameo riding a cruiser bike with a parrot dome.
- **Mega Vegas Elvis.** Headlines in Vegas wearing what Bill insists is his face. Bill wants $50 million for it.
- **Ambient animals:**
  - A raccoon on the bins who finds Bill's satchel "structurally inferior".
  - Rabbits with justified trust issues.
  - Birds with poor navigation.
  - Rats that provoke involuntary scavenger chases.

## The world

- **The estate:**
  - The 1955 kitchen (mint cabinets, chrome, Formica, rose curtains) and the living room.
  - A back hall with an antique vault.
  - Stairs down to the basement synth altar and newspaper hoard.
  - A backyard with a dig patch, stepping stones, a tree, and a picket fence with a gate onto the lane.
- **The Route (Act 2).** A Toronto west-end-style neighbourhood, used for mood rather than accuracy:
  - A laneway behind the house with garages, bins, graffiti, hydro poles wrapped in ivy, wires overhead, and a curbside mattress.
  - A municipal parking lot with a pay machine.
  - The back of a corner variety store, with its dumpster and a cardboard heap.
  - A boxing gym.
  - A main street with a streetcar every 45 seconds and shopfronts: video rentals, laundromat, barber, fruit and veg, café, bakery, hardware, records.
- **Later zones:** the city landfill (Big Wanda), a dangerous old factory, a park at dusk, and a private airfield at the far edge of town.

## What the player can do

Keep ideas inside these verbs, or make them passive (they just happen to Bill):

| Verb | Input | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shuffle | Move | The default walk |
| Hurry | Hold Shift | A panicked run; he skids when he reverses |
| **Toot dash** | Space | A soup-powered fart that launches him forward, with a green cloud and "PFFRRT!". Three charges that refill. The cloud makes Gary gag and knocks the raccoon out cold. |
| Grab | E | Pick up, deliver, and **mash** during challenges |
| Drop | R | Put down what he's carrying |

- **Carrying:** small finds go in a four-slot satchel; big ones (the stump, rails, the grate) are carried in both hands and change how he walks.

## Practical limits

- **Short.** A gag lasts 2–6 seconds and doesn't stop play for long.
- **Readable from far away.** Big body actions, big props, big lettering. No subtle facial expressions and no small text at game zoom.
- **Light on dialogue.** One short Bill line or one narrator line per gag at most. Speech appears in comic balloons, and characters talk in gibberish "voices".
- **Cheap to build is better.** Cheap means:
  - props made from simple shapes;
  - an existing pratfall;
  - clouds, stars and lettering;
  - synthesised sounds;
  - an NPC doing one simple action.

  Expensive means new characters with complex animation, lip-sync, long cutscenes, or realistic physics.
- **No stuck states.** Nothing that blocks progress or needs the player to figure something out.

# Story Brainstorm Brief, Round 2: The Scavenger's Chronicles

*For an outside brainstorming partner (written to be pasted into ChatGPT). 30 September 2026. Round 1 was [gag-brainstorm.md](gag-brainstorm.md).*

## What I'm asking you for

I'm building **The Scavenger's Chronicles**, a browser game: a ridiculously slapstick, crude, interactive comedy about Bill, who has spent forty years preparing to write a synth masterpiece (and a novel) and has finished neither.

This round is about **story, grounded in what you remember**. Round 1 gave us great gags; the ones that landed hardest were the ones only this circle of friends would get. So this time:

1. **Go through your memory first, properly.** Search our past conversations for everything about the people, places, habits and running jokes listed under "What to look for" below.
2. **Write down what you find as a memory ledger**, with how sure you are of each item, before any ideas.
3. **Then brainstorm from the ledger.** Tie every idea you can back to a ledger entry. Invent freely where memory runs out, but label it.

If anything in "Canon so far" contradicts what you remember, say so. Memory beats my guesses.

## Privacy rules for your answer

- **No real addresses, street names or identifying details** about places. "A corner store in Toronto's west end" is fine; a street name is not.
- **Only people in this circle of friends** (the ones the game is already about) by name. Anyone else from memory, such as real neighbours or real former students, becomes a fictional stand-in with a new name. Flag it when you've done that.
- **Keep jokes on logic and obsessions,** not on anyone's real health, money, age, loneliness or looks. If a remembered story is sensitive, tell me it exists in one line and don't use it.

## Canon so far (built, playable)

### The premise

It's 2026. Bill, 54, lives in his late mother's 1955 house, now a hoarder shambles; he calls himself an **archivist**. Upstairs is all 1950s because he never replaces anything; the basement is a 1986 synth altar. He was a **teacher**. His novel, ***Captain Caffeine***, is over two dozen sticky notes collected over thirty years plus one filled page of a lined notepad; the first page is excellent. His motto: "SOUP FIRST. EVERYTHING ELSE LATER." The game ends (not built yet) with a private jet to Las Vegas, where the eight-bar masterpiece finally plays and Mega Vegas Elvis turns out to have Bill's face.

### The look and voice

- **The look:** a ligne claire comic in 3D, from an isometric camera. Bill has long stringy hair, a comb-over, black sunglasses, a plaid flannel shirt, a sweater vest and a satchel.
- **His voice:** a legal letter written by a 14-year-old: deadpan, formal, aggrieved, never embarrassed.
  - "Exhibit A."
  - "My satchel is at capacity. I am consulting counsel." (Counsel is the cat.)
  - "Noted. My legal department will be in touch."
- **Musings he says in the game now:**
  - "The front yard. Wild on purpose. It's a statement. The statement is 'go away'."
  - "My sidewalk. Technically the city's. Morally mine."
  - "The Lug Nutz. I can hear their protein from here."

### The world

- **The house:** the kitchen, the living room, and a front hall with Bill's Legal Department (a typewriter) and the antique vault. The basement has the synth altar and the hoard. There's a back yard with a dig patch and a workbench.
- **The front of the house:** an overgrown front yard (tall weeds, a bathtub planter, a rusty bike, a dry birdbath, "ARCHIVE: KEEP OFF"), a stoop with unread newspapers and a GO AWAY mat, and windows papered over with newsprint.
- **A quiet residential street** of semis on both sides. The camera always faces whichever side Bill is on.
- **The laneway** out back, a parking lot, the corner store (Gary's dumpster behind it), and **LUG NUTZ BOXING & IRON**.
- **Big Wanda's Junkyard** at the end of the lane. It's padlocked ("wanda is at lunch") until the first two errands are done.

## The comedy rules

1. **Easy and guided.** One objective always on screen; nothing to get stuck on; every challenge can be won by mashing. The game is about moving the player through comic events, not about difficulty.
2. **A gag every 10–15 seconds.**
3. **Failing is funnier than winning**, and retries are instant.
4. **Physical slapstick** that reads from far away: big body actions, big props, big comic lettering.
5. **Crude, not cruel.** Farts, burps, garbage, bodily indignity and profanity on signs are fine. Nothing sexual, no slurs, and no jokes about real health, money, age, loneliness or looks.
6. **Bill loses his dignity, never the war.** He always gets a small, absurd victory.

## Practical limits

- **Short beats.** A gag lasts 2–6 seconds, and a challenge 10–30 seconds.
- **Light on dialogue.** One short line per beat. Characters speak in comic balloons with gibberish voices.
- **Cheap to build is better.** Cheap means:
  - props made from simple shapes;
  - existing pratfalls (stagger, faceplant, butt-flop, flung);
  - clouds, stars and lettering;
  - synthesised sounds;
  - NPCs doing one simple action.
- **No puzzles and no stuck states.**
